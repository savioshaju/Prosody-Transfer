\chapter{Cross-Lingual Word Alignment: SimAlign vs. Awesome-Align}
\label{chap:alignment}

In cross-lingual prosody and emphasis transfer, the primary objective is to project an identified emphasis (or focus) constituent from a source utterance (English) into the corresponding syntactic constituent of the translated target utterance (Malayalam). Because English follows a predominantly Subject-Verb-Object (SVO) order with head-initial characteristics, whereas Malayalam follows a flexible, predominantly Subject-Object-Verb (SOV) order with extensive agglutination and case marking, direct linear or heuristic mapping is insufficient. Robust word-level alignment is therefore essential to bridge cross-lingual syntactic divergences and accurately localize the constituent targeted for clefting.

This chapter presents the comparative evaluation of two neural alignment paradigms: \textbf{SimAlign} \citep{sabet2020simalign}, an unsupervised alignment framework leveraging static and contextualized representations from pre-trained multilingual language models, and \textbf{Awesome-Align} \citep{dou2021word}, an alignment framework that fine-tunes multilingual encoders on parallel corpora with explicit word-alignment objectives. Rather than treating both aligners symmetrically, this chapter focuses directly on the operational and linguistic rationale for choosing SimAlign over Awesome-Align, supported by empirical comparative alignments on concrete English-Malayalam sentences and published benchmark metrics from the literature.

\section{Overview of the Evaluated Aligners}

\subsection{SimAlign: Unsupervised Similarity Alignment}
SimAlign \citep{sabet2020simalign} is an unsupervised, zero-shot word alignment framework that extracts word alignments directly from pre-trained multilingual language models (such as \texttt{bert-\allowbreak base-\allowbreak multilingual-\allowbreak cased} or XLM-RoBERTa) without requiring parallel training text or human-annotated word alignments. 

The core mechanism operates in four concise steps:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Embedding Extraction:} Subword representations are extracted from an intermediate layer of the multilingual encoder (empirically, Layer~8 of mBERT provides the optimal semantic-syntactic trade-off).
    \item \textbf{Subword Pooling:} Subword embeddings corresponding to a surface word are average-pooled into a single word vector $\mathbf{v}(w)$.
    \item \textbf{Similarity Matrix:} Pairwise cosine similarities between source and target word vectors form a similarity matrix $S \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$.
    \item \textbf{Decoding via IterMax:} Instead of simple greedy ArgMax, SimAlign implements \textit{IterMax}, an iterative matching algorithm that first selects reciprocal maximal pairs, discounts similarities with already-aligned words, and iteratively extracts secondary alignment links.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Awesome-Align: Fine-Tuned Multilingual Objective Alignment}
Awesome-Align \citep{dou2021word} fine-tunes multilingual encoders (e.g., mBERT) on parallel corpora using self-training and translation language modeling objectives to produce sharpened cross-lingual representations. Alignments are decoded from the resulting attention matrices using directional softmax probability thresholding (typically thresholded at $c = 0.001$) or optimal transport, followed by bidirectional intersection.

\section{Why SimAlign Was Chosen Over Awesome-Align}

In our end-to-end focus projection and clefting pipeline, \textbf{SimAlign} (using mBERT with \textit{IterMax}) was chosen as the primary alignment engine over Awesome-Align. The decision is grounded in three decisive linguistic and operational factors:

\subsection{1. Absence of Parallel Alignment Data and Low-Resource Generalization}
Awesome-Align's primary strength lies in fine-tuning on large, high-quality parallel corpora ($>100\text{k}$ aligned sentence pairs). For English-Malayalam:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Publicly available, gold-standard human word alignments are virtually nonexistent.
    \item Available parallel corpora (such as web-scraped bitexts) contain substantial noise, translation shifts, and colloquial paraphrasing. Fine-tuning an encoder under such conditions risks domain overfitting, representation drift, and severe hallucinations on out-of-domain sentences.
    \item In contrast, SimAlign exploits the general, pre-trained representation space of \texttt{bert-\allowbreak base-\allowbreak multilingual-\allowbreak cased} entirely zero-shot. It requires zero fine-tuning, introduces no parallel-corpus bias, and exhibits stable cross-domain generalization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{2. High-Recall IterMax Matching vs. Threshold-Induced Dropping}
English and Malayalam exhibit fundamentally non-isomorphic syntactic structures: English expresses grammatical relations through distinct analytic prepositions and auxiliaries (\textit{in the house}, \textit{has done}), while Malayalam fuses these into agglutinated nominal cases (\ml{വീട്ടിൽ}) and verbal compounds (\ml{അഭിനയമായിരുന്നു}). 

Awesome-Align decodes alignments via directional softmax probability thresholding. In non-isomorphic or syntactically distant constructions, the probability mass across subwords becomes dispersed, causing valid translation pairs to fall below the probability threshold ($c = 0.001$) and be dropped entirely. As demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:comparative_examples}, Awesome-Align frequently omits critical matrix verbs, adverbs, and direct objects.

SimAlign's \textit{IterMax} solves this problem through competitive, iterative matching: once high-confidence reciprocal matches are identified, their similarity scores are zeroed or discounted, allowing remaining content words (including secondary verbs and adverbs) to be captured in subsequent iterations without being discarded.

\subsection{3. Decoupled Modular Integration with Morphological Analyzers}
By maintaining a frozen, lightweight encoder for alignment, morphological complexities are cleanly offloaded to dedicated symbolic tools like \textit{mlmorph} and custom Sandhi splitters. This modularity ensures fast execution on CPU environments without managing task-specific fine-tuned checkpoints.

\textbf{Linguistic Analysis:}\\
When evaluated as standalone neural aligners without syntactic guidance, both models suffer from the strong \textbf{diagonal positional bias} of mBERT when confronting divergent SVO $\to$ SOV word orders. In English, the matrix verb \textit{gave} appears in position~1, while the temporal adverb \textit{yesterday} appears clause-finally (position~5). In Malayalam, the temporal adverb \ml{ഇന്നലെ} appears in position~1, while the verb \ml{കൊടുത്തു} is clause-final (position~5). Consequently, the positional representations pull position~1 to position~1 and position~5 to position~5.

Crucially, however, their failure modes differ significantly:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Awesome-Align completely drops \textit{gave}:} Because Awesome-Align enforces strict probability thresholding on the cross-attention dot product ($c = 0.001$), the attention weight for \textit{gave} falls below threshold, resulting in zero alignment links for the matrix verb.
    \item \textbf{SimAlign preserves complete token coverage:} SimAlign's \textit{IterMax} extracts reciprocal and secondary matches across all tokens without dropping content.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}

The comparative evaluation demonstrates why SimAlign was chosen over Awesome-Align for cross-lingual prosody transfer in Malayalam:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Zero-Shot Robustness:} SimAlign operates directly on pre-trained multilingual embeddings without requiring parallel training data, preventing the domain drift and hallucinations associated with fine-tuning on noisy low-resource bitexts.
    \item \textbf{Higher Alignment Recall:} SimAlign's \textit{IterMax} algorithm avoids the severe constituent-dropping behavior observed in Awesome-Align's probability thresholding, ensuring that critical matrix verbs and adverbs are captured for clefting.
    \item \textbf{Modular Pipeline Design:} SimAlign provides clean constituent boundaries that integrate seamlessly with symbolic morphological tools like \textit{mlmorph} and Sandhi resolution rules.
\end{enumerate}
